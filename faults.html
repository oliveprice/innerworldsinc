01
Unclear Brand Identity

The logo is an illustrative mark rather than a simple wordmark. That approach can work well for a hospitality brand because it carries personality and atmosphere. The issue is the scale. At its current size the detail becomes difficult to read, so the mark loses its ability to immediately introduce the brand.




Aside from this, the broader brand identity does not appear clearly defined. The colors, typography, and visual elements feel somewhat arbitrary rather than part of an intentional system. It gives the impression that these decisions were made during the construction of the website rather than established beforehand as a cohesive identity (which is more than likely the case).

A strong design system would do quite a lot of work in gaining the trust of a visitor.

02
Missing sense of craft

Right now the site reads as though it was built primarily to meet a functional need. With a more deliberate visual approach, it could communicate the care, warmth, and distinct character that I know the physical location already offers.


The overall experience of the site feels generic and somewhat static. It functions... but it does not convey a strong sense of place or character. For a destination like a mountain inn, the website should do more than present information. It should BEGIN THE EXPERIENCE before the guest ever arrives.


The cabins and surroundings are visually compelling, yet the site does little to bring that atmosphere forward. There is an opportunity to let the environment speak for itself through larger, immersive photography and more intentional visual pacing.

Visitors want to feel what it might be like to stay there. They don't want to just read about it.
