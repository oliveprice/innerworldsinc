Power Through

Transmutation

the Cruel Nature of Metamorphosis

by Olive Price
I. Introduction

What are you supposed to do with overwhelming sorrow, grief, anxiety, pain, anger, heartbreak, suspicion, insecurity, obsessive thought, resentment, fear, dread, jealousy, or shame? These afflictions, so unimaginably capable of governing the mind and your perception of reality, often arise from unexpectedly losing someone you love or a traumatic occurrence that changed the way you see the world.

The Last Day of Pompeii (Karl Bryullov, 1830-1833). Detail fragments (left) show figures from the original composition.

The energy you might have used to set goals and move intentionally toward what you want in life, is now being spent just trying to hold yourself together or mask your feelings away to survive.

The easiest or most natural thing to do, when you find yourself in this position, may be to escape. This comes in many forms. Binge-watching shows or movies, gaming for hours, scrolling mindlessly, drinking, abusing any substance that numbs you, and using other people to feel less lonely. Of course some of these practices are more harmful than others, but they are still all the same at their core.

Or Instead, you may succumb to these entities completely, fully embracing the paralysis they have put you in. You isolate yourself, drown in sadness and manically pick at your wound until it becomes the only thing you see. Perhaps you don't even stay stuck. Maybe you keep moving forward, but on autopilot, numb, and without any sense of purpose.

Whatever you do, it hurts all the same.
